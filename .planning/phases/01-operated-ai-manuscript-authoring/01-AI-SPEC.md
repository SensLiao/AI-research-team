## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid — multi-agent workflow, grounded content generation, deterministic scientific validation, and optional local-first retrieval fallback

**Description:**
An internal research-production system for a director who already has validated papers, notes, and result references. It freezes a manuscript contract, coordinates scoped research and writing agents through a sparse DAG, integrates a native LaTeX project, and delivers readable manuscript artifacts plus independent truth and submission audits. Good output is scientifically traceable, coherent across sections, honest about execution and compilation, compliant with the selected venue, and useful before every advisory polish issue is resolved.

**Critical Failure Modes:**

1. Fabricated, nonexistent, or non-entailing citations; unsupported core claims or numbers; or false experiment/PDF execution claims.
2. Cross-section drift in claims, terminology, notation, result values, figure/table labels, or bibliography closure after parallel authoring.
3. Direct or indirect database writes, unsafe path traversal/asset overwrite, or secret-bearing request details entering artifacts or logs.
4. Search/network failure misreported as absence of prior art, or metadata-only results treated as full-text evidence.
5. Registry-only capability, generated TeX, or scripts-only work presented as operated authoring, a compiled PDF, or completed scientific execution.

## 1b. Domain Context

### Domain vertical and stakes

This capability authors and independently reviews AI/ML research manuscripts intended
primarily for current top conferences, CCF A venues, or a comparably selective journal
family. The target is not generic long-form generation. A strong product must make a
specific scientific contribution legible, bind every important claim to evidence or a
frozen result, survive specialist review, reproduce from disclosed materials where the
paper type permits, and obey the selected venue's current official rules.

The cost of a polished but wrong paper is higher than the cost of an unfinished draft:
invented citations, mismatched numbers, hidden data leakage, unfair baselines, false
execution claims, or broken anonymity can invalidate the work. Conversely, a usable
research draft should not be hidden merely because its prose, heading balance, or visual
polish is unfinished. This is the domain expression of the director's 90% usability and
quality / at most 10% governance weighting.

### Authority and venue fit

The authoring run freezes a dated `venue_profile` before drafting. Authority order is:

1. the selected venue's current official author instructions, call, policy pages, and
   official template;
2. the paper-type contract and project decisions;
3. peer-reviewed scientific-writing evidence and primary open-source project evidence;
4. community conventions, which are advisory only.

Illustrative current official sources for the first profiles are:

- ICML 2026 Author Instructions and format checker:
  https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorInstructions and
  https://papercheck.icml.cc/papercheck.html
- ICLR 2026 Author Guide and official template:
  https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide and
  https://github.com/ICLR/Master-Template
- NeurIPS 2026 Call for Papers and Main Track Handbook:
  https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers and
  https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook
- CCF's official seventh-edition catalog, released 2026-03-31:
  https://www.ccf.org.cn/Academic_Evaluation/By_category/

ICML, ICLR, and NeurIPS rules differ in page limits, required statements/checklists,
anonymity details, and packaging. Those values must live in versioned venue overlays and
must be refreshed from the official source, never inferred from a generic "top venue"
token. CCF classification is a director-facing venue-priority aid, not evidence that a
paper fits a venue or will be accepted; the CCF itself describes the catalog as a
recommendation and distinguishes full/regular papers from other publication types.

### Paper-type contracts

The paper architect selects one primary type and may add typed secondary contributions.
The type changes the evidence plan but never weakens truth gates.

| Paper type | Load-bearing expert expectations |
|---|---|
| Method / algorithm | Precisely scoped novelty, defensible assumptions, algorithmic detail, complexity where relevant, fair strong baselines, ablations that isolate the mechanism, uncertainty and failure analysis. |
| Empirical analysis | Predeclared questions where possible, representative datasets, valid controls, multiple seeds or justified alternatives, uncertainty/effect sizes, sensitivity and robustness, restrained causal language. |
| Dataset / benchmark | Provenance and license, construction/annotation protocol, quality and leakage audit, representativeness and limitations, split design, metric validity, maintenance/governance plan. |
| Systems / tooling | Real end-to-end artifact, architecture and operational boundary, realistic workload, correctness and resource/performance evaluation, reproducible deployment details, comparison to relevant systems. |
| Theory | Explicit definitions and assumptions, correct statements/proofs, relationship to prior results, meaningful regime/examples, limitations of the theorem's applicability. |
| Position / survey | Complete and auditable search/scope method, fair taxonomy, evidence-backed synthesis, contradiction/coverage analysis, clear distinction between fact, interpretation, and proposal. |

### Expert evaluation rubric ingredients

Domain reviewers score the dimensions below on a 1–5 anchored scale. These scores are
advisory for readable drafts; deterministic defects referenced in the right column remain
hard truth or submission gates.

| Dimension | 1 / 3 / 5 anchors | Non-negotiable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution and significance | **1:** no identifiable advance or wrong problem; **3:** useful incremental contribution with bounded impact; **5:** clear, important advance that changes capability or understanding. | Contribution statements are consistent across title, abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion and are not broader than the evidence. |
| Novelty and related work | **1:** material prior art omitted/misrepresented; **3:** relevant lines covered with remaining boundary questions; **5:** collision-tested positioning explains the exact difference and why it matters. | Every novelty claim maps to verified prior-art evidence; search failure is never treated as novelty proof. |
| Technical correctness | **1:** fatal method/proof/design flaw; **3:** broadly correct with repairable gaps; **5:** assumptions, method, derivations, and limitations withstand specialist scrutiny. | No leakage, invalid comparison, contradicted claim, fabricated artifact, or unacknowledged fatal assumption. |
| Empirical design | **1:** inadequate controls or invalid metrics; **3:** credible main study with some missing robustness; **5:** strong controls, justified datasets/metrics, seeds/uncertainty, ablations, robustness and negative results. | All numbers match frozen sources/receipts; splits, preprocessing, baselines, budgets, and selection procedures are disclosed and fair. |
| Reproducibility | **1:** essential method/data details absent; **3:** a skilled researcher can reconstruct most of the work; **5:** complete environment, data/model provenance, commands, seeds, configs, and artifact linkage support faithful reproduction. | Reproducibility claims match actually available materials; planned scripts are not described as executed experiments. |
| Claim-evidence and citations | **1:** important claims unsupported or citations wrong; **3:** main claims supported with localized gaps; **5:** exact-span/result entailment is complete, balanced, and contradiction-aware. | Load-bearing claims close to exact source spans or frozen results; citation identity and bibliography/in-text closure pass. |
| Baseline fairness and ablation | **1:** straw baselines or incompatible budgets; **3:** credible comparisons with a few caveats; **5:** strongest relevant baselines under matched data/tuning/compute plus mechanism-isolating ablations. | No hidden oracle, leakage, incompatible split, selective reporting, or materially unequal tuning budget. |
| Clarity and structure | **1:** contribution cannot be reconstructed; **3:** understandable with editing needs; **5:** concise argument, stable terms/notation, interpretable figures/tables, and clear limits. | Headline claims, definitions, symbols, labels, and cross-references are internally consistent. Style preferences remain advisory. |
| Limitations, ethics, and societal effects | **1:** material risk/limitation concealed; **3:** principal limits disclosed; **5:** concrete failure modes, affected groups, misuse/privacy/license concerns, and scope of safe interpretation are analyzed. | Required venue statements exist; sensitive/private data and licenses are handled within project permissions. |
| Venue and paper-type fit | **1:** wrong audience/type or mandatory policy conflict; **3:** plausible fit with positioning work; **5:** contribution, evidence density, framing, and artifact package match the frozen venue/type contract. | Current official template, anonymity, page/file, checklist, and packaging rules all pass before submission-ready. |

### Domain-specific failure modes

- **Narrative overreach:** abstract or conclusion claims exceed what the reported study
  establishes, especially causal/generalization claims from narrow benchmarks.
- **Prior-art collision:** a cited or omitted paper already performed the same
  method-by-problem intervention and experiment; superficial wording differences are
  presented as novelty.
- **Baseline theater:** weak/obsolete baselines, mismatched data or compute, unequal tuning,
  missing strong public results, or an oracle baseline presented as deployable.
- **Leakage and selection bias:** train/test contamination, benchmark reuse, prompt/example
  leakage, cherry-picked seeds, post-hoc metric or checkpoint selection, or hidden failed
  trials.
- **Number drift:** prose, tables, figures, appendix, and abstract report different values,
  units, aggregation, uncertainty, or dataset split.
- **Citation laundering:** a citation exists but does not entail the sentence; metadata or
  another paper's summary is treated as exact evidence.
- **Reproducibility theater:** commands/configs are listed but inputs, versions, seeds,
  licenses, preprocessing, or execution receipts are absent.
- **Visual persuasion without truth:** truncated axes, incomparable scales, unreadable
  uncertainty, decorative diagrams, or captions that make unsupported causal claims.
- **False execution:** generated scripts, planned experiments, or model-written result
  tables are described as completed scientific evidence.
- **Template/anonymity drift:** an old template, identity-bearing asset/metadata, or an
  unofficial rule is allowed to override the selected venue's current instructions.

### Evaluation roles and compliance boundary

At minimum, evaluation involves: a domain/contribution reviewer; a methods and
reproducibility reviewer; an independent claim/citation auditor; a result-number auditor;
a figure/table reviewer; and a venue/LaTeX compliance reviewer. One person/agent may hold
multiple compatible roles for a small paper, but the manuscript integrator cannot be the
sole independent reviewer. Submission candidates retain a human director gate.

There is no single universal regulation governing AI manuscript prose. Applicable hard
obligations are project- and venue-specific: research integrity, privacy/consent where
human or sensitive data are involved, dataset/code/model licenses, copyright and
attribution, disclosure policies, anonymity, and required ethics/impact/reproducibility
statements. The run records which obligations apply; absence must be explicit rather than
silently assumed.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Native Research Agent Teams operated-mode framework

**Version:** In-repository contract v1 for this phase; every run and build freezes the applicable Git/source hashes rather than relying on an unpinned external framework release.

**Rationale:**
The existing Python control plane already supplies frozen task frames, dependency-authorized worker waves, resumable checkpoints, hash-chained run state, deterministic reducers, provider-neutral model routing, and the bounded read-only vault seam required by this phase. Adding a second agent framework would duplicate the graph, persistence, and truth model and would weaken rather than improve auditability.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph | Best third-party fit for stateful graph/checkpointing, but acceptable only as a full future control-plane replacement; coexistence would create two sources of truth and a new persistence boundary. |
| CrewAI | Convenient role abstraction, but its coarser state/error/checkpoint model does not match the repository's dependency receipts, deterministic reducers, and strict scientific audit boundary. |
| LlamaIndex / Haystack | Strong retrieval or pipeline tools, but retrieval is not the missing product and the existing recall/search ports already own that seam. |
| Provider-native agent SDKs | Conflict with model-agnostic routing and add vendor lock-in without replacing the repository's validated scheduler/run store. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No. Concrete model IDs and providers remain deployment bindings outside the research architecture.

No new vector database, embedding model, tracing service, or evaluation framework is required.

| Need | Phase 1 integration |

### 3.5 Open-source borrow/reject map

| System | Borrow | Reject or constrain |
|---|---|---|
| PaperOrchestra | Outline; literature and plotting in parallel; section writing; refinement; explicit artifacts; versioned TeX/PDF; compile-before-accept and rollback. | Metadata citation maps are not entailment proof; retrieval failure cannot become an empty corpus; LLM reviewer score cannot release; never delete/rebuild the run directory. Pin a repository commit because no stable release exists. |
| AI Scientist v2 | Bounded branches for hard local problems, execution journals, revision checkpoints, compile/figure inspection as advisory QA. | No autonomous shell/package/web execution, unbounded tree search, model-selected final paper, hardcoded provider/model, or inference from paper-like output to real execution. |
| Agent Laboratory | Human feedback checkpoints, phase boundaries, scaffold-first drafting, one downstream integrator, compile/revert loop. | No pickled whole-workflow state, regex reviewer JSON, LLM reward as truth, metadata search as citation support, repository API keys, or unrestricted execution. |
| STORM | Perspective-guided questions, outline before prose, modular curation/outline/writing, local-corpus adapter. | No web/Wikipedia-first authority, source-level citation in place of exact support, or publication-ready claim. |
| PaperQA2 | Local search → gather → rerank/contextualize → answer, abstention, and more retrieved candidates than final sources. | Generated chunks/summaries are discovery aids only; citations still require an existence-checked snapshot and claim-level page/section/span support. |

### 3.6 Specific pitfalls

1. A YAML entry is not operation. The Python recipe, registry mirror, schemas, reducers, tests, and operated evidence must all exist.
2. `parallel_groups` do not create concurrency. An external harness executes only the wave authorized by `schedule_next_wave`.
3. Group barriers can accidentally serialize a sparse graph; prefer explicit `depends_on` with `group_barriers=False` unless a scientific barrier is required.
4. Schema-valid output can still be scientifically false; exact-citation, numeric, execution, and venue truth need independent gates.
5. Generated TeX is not a compiled paper. Only a real process receipt and hashed PDF may set `COMPILED`.
6. Papers, webpages, repositories, and LaTeX comments are untrusted data; their instructions never alter tools, permissions, or vault boundaries.
7. Windows and Linux differ in quoting, Unicode paths, TeX discovery, case behavior, and PDF file locks; both must be tested.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### 4.1 Official-rule authority and frozen contract

Resolve requirements in this order:

1. current-year official venue author guide, handbook, and track call;
2. current-year official template/example files;
3. official checker, submission form, and mandatory checklist;
4. frozen project/run contract and director decisions;
5. advisory community conventions.

At `begin`, freeze every applicable rule source with URL/local ref, retrieval time, SHA-256, venue/year/track, and applicability; freeze the official template tree separately. Higher authority wins a conflict, and the conflict is recorded. An old/offline snapshot may support a readable draft with caveats but cannot support current-year submission readiness.

The `manuscript_contract` pins project/run, north star, paper type, venue/year/track, anonymity state, permitted claims, source/result/figure allow-lists and hashes, execution status, rule/template snapshots, Paper Design Tokens, section/claim plan, terminology/notation, bibliography policy, build/review policy, privacy restrictions, and Done When gates. Venue page limits, disclosure, anonymity, checklist, and template rules remain versioned data, never Python constants.

### 4.2 Native sparse DAG, state, and checkpoints

Use the existing forward stage path:

```text
DISCOVER -> DESIGN -> ANALYZE -> VERIFY -> REPORT
```

Recommended dependency graph:

```text
venue-profile ─┐
local-coverage ├─> manuscript-contract ─> outline/claim-plan ─┬─> introduction ──┐
result-audit ──┘                                             ├─> methods ───────┤
                                                             ├─> results ───────┼─> single-integrator
                                                             └─> related-work ──┘

single-integrator -> deterministic build -> independent audits -> meta-review
                  -> targeted supplements or director report
```

Use explicit `depends_on` and `group_barriers=False` for independent branches. A worker reads one frozen common snapshot plus declared predecessor slices, never “latest.” Each wave is authorized, executed externally, validated, hash-committed, and checkpointed before dependents are released. Resume uses committed artifacts, not model conversation memory.

Failed roles produce immutable, hash-linked supplements. Refresh only the failed role and explicit consumers. Formatting normalization uses no research worker. Any canonical manuscript change creates a new manuscript hash and invalidates reviews bound to the previous hash.

### 4.3 Local-first coverage and gap routing

Measure coverage separately for comparison, method, implementation, dataset, metric, and industry/application evidence. Each dimension ends as:

- `sufficient`: local evidence meets the declared criterion;
- `deficit`: a named missing concept, date range, venue, or claim type plus query plan;
- `provider_failure`: fallback could not complete;
- `unverified`: evidence is too weak to decide.

Only `deficit` authorizes the existing `paper_search` fallback. `provider_failure` and `unverified` never mean “no prior art.” Search metadata proves existence and aids triage but has `claim_support: none`. Before bibliography admission, bind an accessible source snapshot, hash, bibliographic identity, and exact page/section/span to the manuscript claim. If full text cannot be lawfully verified, weaken/qualify the claim or omit it.

Retrieve and rerank more candidates than the final context permits; admit only strong, non-duplicate evidence. This borrows PaperQA2's discipline without delegating citation truth to generated summaries or adding a vector stack.

### 4.4 Paper Design Token cascade

Resolve deterministic presentation tokens as:

```text
base -> paper-type -> venue/year/track -> project -> frozen run override
```

Tokens cover geometry, typography, heading depth, columns, theorem/algorithm/table/figure conventions, captions, color/accessibility, bibliography, anonymity, appendix/supplement, disclosure/checklist placement, and build engine. Every resolved value retains its layer and source hash.

Lower layers may specialize advisory values but cannot weaken official hard rules. Conflicting project/run overrides fail contract creation. Keep semantic content separate from design tokens: workers propose structured content and native LaTeX fragments; the integrator applies the official template/macros. A parallel HTML/Markdown rendering is review convenience, not venue-compliant source.

### 4.5 Structured bundles and one canonical integrator

Every section worker emits `manuscript_section_bundle` containing:

- `contract_version`, `worker_role`, `section_id`, `manuscript_snapshot_sha256`;
- `input_refs[]` with reference, SHA-256, and slice kind;
- `claim_uses[]` mapping claim IDs to evidence/result refs;
- `draft_latex`, citation keys, labels, cross-references, figure/table refs, notation uses;
- uncertainties, omissions, requested supplements, and `content_hash`.

Workers cannot edit `main.tex`, `references.bib`, canonical figures, or another bundle. The single integrator verifies hashes, reconciles terminology/notation/claims/numbers/labels/citations, applies the official template and token cascade, rejects unknown refs, writes the canonical source tree atomically, and emits a new immutable manuscript hash. It may request a targeted supplement but may not invent evidence, results, or citations.

### 4.6 LaTeX and figure truth

Build the exact canonical snapshot in a run-scoped directory. Preferred command:

```text
latexmk -norc -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error -file-line-error -recorder -outdir=<run-build> main.tex
```

Call `shutil.which("latexmk")`, then `subprocess.run(argv, shell=False, cwd=source_dir, timeout=bounded_seconds)`. `-norc` prevents user rc drift; `-recorder` supports an input/output manifest. Never enable shell escape. Record executable/version, argv, OS, TeX engine, return code, log refs, source/template hashes, recorder manifest, PDF hash, duration, and timeout.

Build state is exactly:

- `COMPILED`: process succeeded, required references resolved, PDF exists, and its hash is recorded;
- `TOOLCHAIN_MISSING`: latexmk, required engine/package/bibliography tool, or official template is unavailable;
- `COMPILE_FAILED`: tooling ran but TeX, bibliography, reference, timeout, or policy checks failed.

Only `COMPILED` exposes `main.pdf`. A deterministic bounded multipass engine fallback is allowed only when the official template requires and tests it; a one-pass `pdflatex` call is not.

Every figure/table asset records immutable source-data/result refs and hashes, generation script/notebook ref and hash (or explicit external provenance), creator/time/parameters/runtime, license/permission, caption claim IDs, numeric source cells, label, asset hash, and accessibility text. A plotting/image model may propose a visual, but synthetic or unverifiable result figures cannot pass the truth gate.

### 4.7 Independent review

Run review as a separate recipe against a frozen manuscript/PDF hash. Scientific, exact-citation, numeric/result, LaTeX/figure, anonymity/privacy, and venue reviewers see the common contract and scoped evidence, not one another's conclusions. A deterministic meta-review joins their immutable verdicts.

Generation artifacts are not independent evidence. Citation audit reopens exact source spans; numeric audit traces values to frozen result/table refs; execution audit distinguishes receipt-bound results from planned/scripts-only work; venue audit checks frozen official rules/template/checklist/checker; build audit verifies the actual PDF and recorder receipt; a second-reader critique must be reconciled. Advisory style defects yield supplements/caveats. Fabricated core sources, unsupported core claims, false execution/compile claims, leakage, invalid comparisons, permission violations, or corrupt input fail closed.

### 4.8 Provider-neutral model and tool configuration

| Work | Capability | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage classification, query expansion, schema repair, mechanical summary | `reasoning_strength: strong`, `provider: any` | Lowest supported randomness; explicit output-token ceiling. |
| Architecture, section synthesis, integration | `reasoning_strength: frontier`, `long_context: true`, `provider: any` | Low randomness (target 0.1–0.3 when supported); explicit ceiling. |
| Citation, numeric, execution, and meta-review | `reasoning_strength: frontier`, `tool_use: true`, `provider: any` | Lowest supported randomness; await complete structured output. |

Concrete deployment bindings come only from `RAT_RUNTIME_MODEL`, `RAT_RUNTIME_REASONING_EFFORT`, and `RAT_RUNTIME_SERVICE_TIER`. Never hardcode model IDs. Unsupported sampling parameters are omitted by the provider adapter, not approximated in scientific logic.

Tools are least-privilege: read-only vault references, explicit local snapshots, fallback discovery after a deficit, and isolated LaTeX build. No arbitrary shell, package install, uncontrolled egress, destructive output rebuild, secret store, or vault write.

### 4.9 Context slicing

After reserving explicit output tokens and a safety margin, start with:

- 15% frozen contract, outline, terminology, notation, and design tokens;
- 55% reranked claim/evidence/result spans for the section;
- 20% validated predecessor bundles and cross-section interfaces;
- 10% tool/serialization headroom.

On overflow, remove advisory context first, deduplicate, rerank by claim coverage/source quality, then truncate at document boundaries. Summaries are `noncitable_context` and never replace exact spans. The integrator sees validated bundles and closure tables, not the raw corpus.

### 4.10 Windows/Linux pitfalls

- Pass subprocess argv as a list with `shell=False`; never interpolate request/project text into a shell command.
- Resolve targets inside the run directory and store portable relative paths with forward slashes.
- Do not rely on `~`, `$HOME`, drive-relative paths, case sensitivity, symlinks, or global TeX rc files.
- Test Unicode/space-containing paths and long paths on Windows; use a short run-scoped build path where TeX requires it.
- PDF viewers can lock files on Windows; close them before replacement or version output names.
- Normalize newline/encoding at integration and compile the same frozen fixture on Windows and Linux CI.
- Record OS, Python, latexmk, TeX distribution/engine, and template hashes for drift diagnosis.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured outputs: authoritative JSON Schema, optional Pydantic v2

The authoritative runtime remains the repository's Draft 2020-12 JSON Schema registry and `validate_payload(artifact_type, payload)`. Add `manuscript_section_bundle.schema.json` and register it in `PAYLOAD_SCHEMAS`. Pydantic v2 is only an optional typed adapter when already provided by a future pinned environment; this example does **not** authorize a new dependency.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, model_validator

from research_agent_teams.tools.validate_artifact import validate_payload


class InputRef(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", strict=True)

    ref: str = Field(min_length=1)
    sha256: str = Field(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{64}$")
    slice_kind: Literal[
        "global_contract",
        "claim_evidence",
        "dependency_bundle",
        "venue_rule",
        "asset",
    ]


class ClaimUse(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", strict=True)

    claim_id: str = Field(min_length=1)
    evidence_refs: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    result_refs: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def require_support(self) -> "ClaimUse":
        if not self.evidence_refs and not self.result_refs:
            raise ValueError("claim needs evidence_refs or result_refs")
        return self


class SectionDraftBundle(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", strict=True)

    contract_version: Literal["1.0"]
    worker_role: str = Field(min_length=1)
    section_id: str = Field(min_length=1)
    manuscript_snapshot_sha256: str = Field(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{64}$")
    input_refs: list[InputRef] = Field(min_length=1)
    claim_uses: list[ClaimUse] = Field(default_factory=list)
    draft_latex: str = Field(min_length=1)
    citation_keys: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    labels: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    figure_refs: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    uncertainties: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    content_hash: str = Field(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{64}$")


def validate_section_bundle(raw: dict) -> dict:
    # Convenience typing only; JSON Schema is the cross-runtime authority.
    typed = SectionDraftBundle.model_validate(raw, strict=True)
    payload = typed.model_dump(mode="json")
    errors = validate_payload("manuscript_section_bundle", payload)
    if errors:
        raise ValueError("; ".join(errors))
    return payload
```

Request provider-native structured JSON where available, then validate locally regardless. Permit at most **two structural repair retries**. Feed only validation errors, schema, and prior structured payload into the repair; do not invite a scientific rewrite. Log attempt, validator path/type, prompt/schema/input hashes, capability tier, latency, and token counts—never secrets or unrestricted source bodies. Structural errors may retry; missing evidence, unsupported numbers, and contradictions require targeted supplements. After two failures, mark `NEEDS_SUPPLEMENT` and block dependents.

### 4b.2 Async-first execution without hidden concurrency

The spine and scheduler are synchronous authorization/state functions. The external harness:

1. calls `schedule_next_wave`;
2. concurrently dispatches only returned dependency-safe workers;
3. awaits each complete structured response;
4. validates, hashes, and commits each bundle/receipt;
5. calls the scheduler again.

Do not call `asyncio.run()` inside FastAPI, a notebook, or another active event loop; await from that loop. A synchronous CLI may own one top-level `asyncio.run(main())`. Bound concurrency by provider and filesystem limits. Stream progress for human UX only; await complete JSON for validation and never persist a partial stream as an artifact.

### 4b.3 Prompt discipline

- **System:** immutable role, tool/read/write scope, scientific invariants, output schema, abstention behavior, and forbidden actions.
- **User:** frozen task contract plus authorized hashed context manifest.
- **Untrusted data:** wrap webpages, papers, READMEs, LaTeX comments, notes, and tool output in a fresh high-entropy delimiter and state that embedded instructions are non-authoritative.
- **Few-shot:** keep one or two compact schema/edge examples inline where needed. Dynamic examples come only from vetted local artifacts, are hashed, and are labeled `example_only` so claims/citations cannot leak.
- **Budgets:** set explicit maximum output tokens for every role; bind reasoning/output controls at deployment when supported. No unbounded production calls.
- **Review separation:** keep reviewer conclusions out of author prompts until the verdict is frozen; send only the targeted repair contract afterward.

### 4b.4 Context-window management

Let `W` be the deployed context window, `R` the explicit output reservation, and `S` a provider/tool safety margin. Admit at most `W - R - S` input tokens before dispatch.

Rerank spans by required claim coverage, source quality, exact-support availability, and non-duplication. Use Section 4.9's 15/55/20/10 split as a starting policy and record actual slice sizes. Summaries may navigate background but remain `noncitable_context`. Resume compacts state into validated artifacts plus hashes, never a free-form conversation summary. On overflow, drop advisory examples/style notes and lower-ranked candidates first; never silently truncate venue rules, allowed-result boundaries, or support for a retained claim.

### 4b.5 Cost and latency budget

Concrete dollars depend on deployment bindings and their current price table; hardcoding a model ID or stale price would violate the framework choice. Freeze prices at run start and compute:

```text
estimated_cost =
  sum(input_tokens_by_tier * input_price_by_tier
    + output_tokens_by_tier * output_price_by_tier)
  + retrieval/build service cost

estimated_latency =
  sequential_critical_path_latency
  + deterministic build/review latency
```

Initial planning assumption: **18–26 model calls per manuscript** across contract/coverage/outline, 4–8 section/specialist bundles, integration, 6–10 independent audits/meta-review, and bounded repair. The final plan must replace this with its actual DAG and per-run call, token, cost, retry, and wall-clock ceilings.

Exact-match cache only immutable work, keyed by worker-spec, schema, model-binding, prompt, ordered-input, and tool-policy hashes. Semantic caching is limited to non-claim routing, query expansion, and advisory summaries; it is forbidden for final prose, claims, citations, numbers, integration, build truth, and audits. Use `strong` capability for narrow classification/query/format tasks and `frontier` for architecture, synthesis/integration, and truth audits. Every receipt records tokens, duration, retries, cache status/key, tool time, and logical capability; stop before a hard budget is exceeded.

### 4b.6 Human accountability and disclosure

AI tools are not authors. Human authors remain responsible for every claim, citation, figure, reference, disclosure, and submission. Freeze the venue/year policy and emit required disclosure/checklist artifacts. Current ICLR guidance requires disclosure of significant LLM involvement; current NeurIPS guidance keeps responsibility for AI-assisted text/figures/references with humans and warns about hallucinated citations and prompt injection. Treat these as versioned examples—always apply the frozen current-year official rule for the selected venue.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### 5.1 Evaluation policy

Evaluation is evidence-first and model-neutral. The default stack is the existing
tamper-evident run store and ledger, authoritative JSON Schemas, deterministic Python
checks, `pytest`, build receipts, and the human-facing files under `director-review/`.
No new tracing service, vector database, or eval platform is required. LLM judges and
domain reviewers may score scientific quality, but they cannot overrule deterministic
truth, permission, provenance, or venue-compliance failures.

Two release questions are evaluated separately:

1. **Readable-draft usability:** may be `USABLE_WITH_CAVEATS` when the remaining defects
   concern prose, organization, visual polish, or non-mandatory coverage.
2. **Submission readiness:** requires every deterministic truth and official-venue check
   to pass, a real PDF build when the venue requires a PDF, and an independent review
   artifact. An advisory score can never manufacture `SUBMISSION_READY`.

### 5.2 Evaluation dimensions and rubrics

| Dimension | Evidence and scoring rule | Readable-draft threshold | Submission threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orchestration integrity | Recompute the sparse DAG; every started node has satisfied declared dependencies, one terminal bundle, immutable input hashes, and a legal state transition. Checkpoint replay must reproduce the same resolved inputs. | No orphaned or unauthorized node; targeted failure may remain explicit. | 100% legal transitions; all required nodes terminal and ledger chain valid. |
| Local-first retrieval | `local-literature-coverage.json` records corpus refs and six gap classes. Network search is legal only for an explicit unresolved gap; search failure is distinct from `NO_EVIDENCE_AFTER_VALID_SEARCH`. | Coverage report exists; unresolved gaps are visible. | Every load-bearing gap is closed or explicitly limits the claim; no undeclared network route. |
| Download boundary | Tool trace and artifacts contain no OpenAlex/content/bulk/PDF download action. OpenAlex may appear only as metadata from the existing search adapter. | Zero prohibited actions. | Zero prohibited actions. |
| Design-token resolution | Re-resolve `base -> paper_type -> venue -> project -> run`; compare canonical JSON and hash with the frozen snapshot. Official hard rules cannot be overridden downstream. | Resolved tokens and provenance exist; advisory conflicts may be reported. | Exact canonical match; no hard-rule override or unknown mandatory token. |
| Structured worker outputs | Validate every bundle against authoritative repository JSON Schema before it becomes dependency-visible. At most two format-only repair attempts, both ledgered. | Invalid optional bundle may become a caveat when downstream truth is unaffected. | All required bundles valid; no silent coercion or third repair. |
| Context authorization | Each section-author input contains the frozen shared snapshot plus only declared dependency slices; hash all delivered slices. | No unauthorized context. | No unauthorized context and all required slices present. |
| Integration coherence | A single `manuscript-integrator` owns the assembled source. Deterministic checks cover terminology, notation, labels, citations, and numbers; independent reviewers score cross-section argument continuity on 1–5. | Deterministic closure passes; median advisory coherence >= 3, lower scores become caveats. | Deterministic closure passes; median coherence >= 4 with no reviewer score below 3. |
| Claim-evidence faithfulness | Every load-bearing claim in abstract/body/conclusion maps to evidence refs and exact spans or frozen result refs. Independent audit labels `ENTAILED`, `PARTIAL`, `CONTRADICTED`, or `UNVERIFIED`. | No contradicted or fabricated claim; partial/unverified claims are weakened or visibly caveated. | 100% load-bearing claims `ENTAILED`; zero unsupported headline claims. |
| Numeric/result truth | Extract manuscript numbers and compare exact value, unit, aggregation, split, and uncertainty against frozen result records and non-LLM receipts. | Zero false-execution claims; mismatches block affected claims. | 100% exact or explicitly derived with a reproducible derivation record. |
| Bibliography/cross-reference closure | BibTeX keys and in-text citations are bidirectionally closed; labels/refs resolve; source existence and bibliographic identity are checked. | No invented citation; unresolved non-load-bearing citation is a caveat. | Zero missing/unused load-bearing citation and zero unresolved cross-reference. |
| Figure/table provenance | Every included asset has source/result refs, owner, content hash, caption/label owner, render command when generated, and non-overwrite proof for director assets. | Provenance complete for every visible asset; visual polish may be a caveat. | Provenance complete, claims match source data, all mandatory captions/labels and venue rules pass. |
| Venue and anonymity compliance | Evaluate the frozen official venue profile: current template, page/file limits, anonymity, required sections/statements, and submission packaging. Community conventions are advisory only. | Missing submission-only items may be caveats if the draft is clearly not submission-ready. | 100% mandatory official checks pass against the recorded rules snapshot. |
| LaTeX/PDF build truth | Detect engine; run safe argv with timeout and shell escape disabled; record command, versions, exit code, logs, hashes, and output existence. | Complete source tree plus honest `TOOLCHAIN_MISSING` or `BUILD_FAILED` is deliverable. | Build status `BUILT`, PDF exists at the declared path, hash recorded, and no unresolved fatal/warning class designated by the venue profile. |
| Independent review separation | Review/rebuttal uses a distinct operated recipe/run and frozen manuscript hash; the authoring integrator cannot act as the only reviewer. | Internal audits exist; independent review may still be pending and visible. | Independent review packet exists and every blocking finding is resolved or director-accepted. |
| Safety and vault boundary | Scan paths, URLs, logs, TeX, BibTeX, and packets for secrets; validate all paths remain inside the run; assert the PhD-Research-OS tree is unchanged. | Zero secret/path/vault violation. | Zero secret/path/vault violation. |
| Status calibration | Derive status from deterministic findings and advisory scores; compare against gold fixture labels. | 100% precision on `BLOCK`; no safe draft hidden for cosmetic reasons. | 100% precision on `SUBMISSION_READY`; no hard failure can be downgraded. |

### 5.3 Reference dataset and fixtures

The version-one gold set contains **at least 16 hermetic cases**. Each case stores a
frozen input manifest, expected tool calls, expected bundles, expected hard findings,
expected readability/submission statuses, and a short human rationale. Synthetic papers
are used for destructive/security cases; one small, license-compatible AI-paper fixture
is used for end-to-end structure and real compilation. No test writes to the vault.

| Fixture family | Minimum cases | Required behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| Local corpus | 2 | Sufficient local coverage performs zero network calls; a concrete comparison/method gap invokes only the existing search port. |
| Retrieval truth | 2 | Search outage produces `SEARCH_FAILED`, never evidence absence; successful exhaustive query may produce `NO_EVIDENCE_AFTER_VALID_SEARCH` with trace. |
| Token/snapshot | 2 | Overlay precedence is deterministic; attempted run override of an official hard rule is rejected. Resume with changed upstream hash invalidates only affected descendants. |
| Bundles/DAG | 2 | Malformed worker output repairs at most twice; unauthorized dependency or orphan node fails closed. |
| Scientific truth | 3 | Unsupported claim, citation identity/entailment mismatch, and numeric/receipt mismatch are each detected; false GPU/execution language hard-blocks. |
| Assets and paths | 2 | Traversal/out-of-run output is rejected; director-owned asset cannot be silently overwritten and retains its original hash. |
| Build | 2 | Missing engine returns `TOOLCHAIN_MISSING` with complete source; installed engine produces a real hashed PDF or an honest `BUILD_FAILED`. |
| Product separation | 1 | Review-only run consumes a frozen manuscript hash, produces no authoring mutation, and emits its own review/rebuttal packet. |
| End to end | 1 | Authoring fixture emits the full source tree, coverage/plan/quality/reviewer/checklist outputs, machine bundles, and correctly derived delivery states. |

Human labels are created by one AI-research domain reviewer and one methods/reproducibility
reviewer; disagreements on scientific claims are reconciled by a third reviewer or the
director. Changes to a gold label require a rationale and dataset-version bump.

### 5.4 Test and CI integration

- Unit/property tests cover token merge order, canonical hashing, state transitions,
  schema validation, path boundaries, citation/label extraction, status derivation, and
  secret-safe error rendering.
- Contract tests replay worker bundles and assert dependency visibility, repair limits,
  search-port calls, and authoring/review separation.
- The hermetic 16-case set runs without network or a real vault. A conditional local
  toolchain test runs only when a supported LaTeX engine is present and must never turn
  absence into a false pass.
- A phase cannot claim operated status until the end-to-end fixture has command evidence,
  the registry/PLATFORM-FACTS mirror tests pass, and the director-review packet is rendered.
- LLM/human rubric evaluations are stored as advisory evidence with evaluator identity,
  prompt/rubric version, model binding (when applicable), and manuscript hash.

## 6. Guardrails

### 6.1 Run-time hard guardrails

These checks run before a bundle becomes dependency-visible and cannot be bypassed by an
LLM score:

- reject secret-bearing URLs, logs, TeX/BibTeX, packets, or exception strings;
- reject paths escaping the run root, symlink/reparse-point escapes, unowned overwrite,
  direct vault writes, and any implicit `/promote-to-vault` action;
- reject OpenAlex/content/bulk/PDF download tool calls in this mode;
- reject invalid required bundles after two format-only repairs;
- reject missing source refs, contradicted/load-bearing unsupported claims, invented
  citations, numeric/result mismatch, or false execution language;
- reject a `BUILT` claim unless a real PDF exists and its hash/build receipt are present;
- reject a submission-ready claim when any official venue/anonymity/template requirement
  fails or the rules snapshot is missing/stale;
- reject dependency access not authorized by the sparse DAG and mutations by any writer
  other than the single integrator after section freeze.

Hard failures derive `BLOCK` for the affected product. They must retain readable evidence
and repair instructions unless the input is irrecoverably corrupt or disclosure itself
would expose a secret.

### 6.2 Advisory quality flywheel

Prose rhythm, heading choices, narrative emphasis, caption polish, optional visual density,
and most section-length guidance remain advisory Paper Design Tokens. Reviewers score
novelty framing, argument continuity, clarity, reproducibility exposition, limitations,
and venue fit. Low scores trigger targeted suggestions or `USABLE_WITH_CAVEATS`, not a
wholesale rerun. Only failed roles and their declared downstream consumers are refreshed;
unchanged bundles remain hash-linked and reusable.

Prompt inputs explicitly label untrusted paper text, retrieved text, and reviewer comments
as data. They cannot change tools, permissions, token hard rules, role identity, or output
schema. Generated code/shell is never executed by a reasoning worker in manuscript mode.

## 7. Production Monitoring

### 7.1 Evidence locations

Each operated run records state transitions and hashes in the existing run store/ledger;
machine metrics and findings remain in validated bundles, while the human entry point is
`director-review/00-REVIEW-PACKET.md`. The authoring product additionally exposes:

- `director-review/manuscript/00-OVERVIEW.md`
- `director-review/manuscript/local-literature-coverage.md`
- `director-review/manuscript/authoring-plan.md`
- `director-review/manuscript/latex/`
- `director-review/manuscript/quality-report.md`
- `director-review/manuscript/reviewer-report.md`
- `director-review/manuscript/submission-checklist.md`
- a real PDF only when the build receipt says `BUILT`.

### 7.2 Per-run metrics and alerts

| Metric | Alert condition |
|---|---|
| required node/bundle completion | Any required node missing, duplicate terminal bundle, illegal transition, or ledger/hash-chain break: immediate hard alert. |
| schema repair attempts | More than 2 for any bundle: hard alert; repair rate above 10% across the last 20 runs: quality alert. |
| local-first/search routing | Network call without explicit gap, any prohibited downloader call, or search failure labeled as absence: immediate hard alert. |
| evidence closure | Any contradicted/fabricated load-bearing claim, invented citation, numeric mismatch, or false-execution claim: immediate hard alert. |
| citation/label/notation closure | Any unresolved submission artifact: hard submission alert; readable-draft caveats tracked by count and section. |
| build truth | `BUILT` without PDF/hash/receipt: immediate hard alert; `TOOLCHAIN_MISSING` tracked as environment status, not model failure. |
| venue compliance | Any mandatory failure or rules snapshot older than its declared review date at submission check: hard submission alert. |
| status calibration | Any known hard failure paired with `USABLE`/`SUBMISSION_READY`, or cosmetic-only defect paired with `BLOCK`: calibration alert. |
| context/cost | Per-role input/output tokens, latency, retries, cache hits, and capability profile are recorded; >2x the rolling median for the same role/profile triggers an advisory efficiency alert. |
| provider neutrality | Record requested capability profile and resolved deployment binding; missing binding evidence or architecture-embedded model ID triggers configuration alert. |

### 7.3 Sampling and review cadence

- Deterministic checks run on every run and every repaired bundle.
- Independent citation/number/venue/build audits run on every submission candidate.
- Until 20 operated authoring runs exist, a human reviews every final packet. Afterwards,
  review remains mandatory for every submission candidate and a minimum 20% stratified
  sample of non-submission readable drafts (including all caveat classes).
- Weekly during active use, aggregate status distribution, hard-failure categories,
  repair/retry rates, search invocation rate, build outcomes, token/latency medians, and
  reviewer disagreements. Any threshold breach opens a targeted fixture/regression task;
  thresholds are never weakened merely to make a release pass.
- Persist evaluator/rubric/token/schema/venue-profile versions so historical runs remain
  interpretable after the policies evolve.
